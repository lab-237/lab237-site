About LAB 237

LAB 237 is an independent, research-driven initiative in Cameroon. We work on artificial intelligence, natural language processing, speech technology and open research resources for Cameroon and similar multilingual settings.

We release our datasets, models and code with clear licences and documentation, enabling other researchers to inspect, reproduce and build on our work.

Tiani Pekins Ebika

Founder, LAB 237

Tiani is a native speaker of Cameroon Pidgin English and Camfranglais. He founded LAB 237 to advance AI research around how Cameroonians speak, write and work, spanning speech technology, document understanding, text-to-SQL and small language models. Separately, he is developing LocalHands Africa, a voice-first service-exchange platform.

Frequently asked questions

What people ask most about CamSpokenNLI and the varieties behind it.

What is the difference between Cameroon Pidgin English and Camfranglais?

Cameroon Pidgin English is an English-based contact language, spoken in some form by an estimated half of Cameroonians. Camfranglais is a separate mixed variety whose vocabulary draws on French, English, Pidgin and Cameroonian languages. Both are mainly spoken rather than written.

What is spoken NLI, and why use speech?

Natural language inference (NLI) asks whether one sentence follows from, contradicts, or is neutral toward another. In CamSpokenNLI the first sentence, the premise, is a recording, because these varieties are mostly spoken.

How well do today’s AI systems handle these varieties?

Not reliably yet. A fine-tuned text model scores about 45% on Pidgin and 46% on Camfranglais in a three-way task where chance is 33%, and a model that sees only the hypothesis does about as well, so we do not claim these scores show reasoning over the sentence pair. Off-the-shelf Whisper speech recognition is far from solved: its best word error rate is 66.6% for Pidgin and 45.5% for Camfranglais, reached only when the matching language is forced in advance.

Is CamSpokenNLI a benchmark?

No. It is a pilot: 114 items per variety, one speaker, one annotator and no held-out test set. It is a seed for larger, multi-speaker versions.

Who made the data, and where do the sentences come from?

The founder, a native speaker of both varieties, wrote and recorded all of it. The scenarios come from everyday life in Buea and Molyko: electricity, water, transport, markets, school and mobile money. The recordings were made on a mobile phone.

Is LAB 237 connected to LocalHands Africa?

CamSpokenNLI was developed alongside LocalHands Africa, a voice-first service-exchange concept by the same founder. They are separate projects, and no data in v1.0 comes from LocalHands Africa.

What comes next?

A larger v2.0 is planned: more speakers and regions, independent annotators, spoken hypotheses, and held-out development and test sets.
